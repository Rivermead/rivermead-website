Pitfalls & dangers

The Risks Most Businesses Underestimate

Accuracy

Invented facts and citations

AI tools can produce confident, plausible and entirely false statements, including authorities that do not exist. Every output that matters needs checking against its source.

Data

Confidential and personal data

Information typed into public tools may leave your control. Personal data engages UK GDPR, including the rules on automated decision-making and the need for a data protection impact assessment.

Regulation

Regulatory exposure

Regulated firms remain responsible for outcomes, whatever tool produced them: FCA Consumer Duty, SRA professional duties, and the EU AI Act for businesses serving EU customers.

Fairness

Bias and discrimination

Models trained on historic data can reproduce its unfairness. Decisions about people must be tested for bias and remain explainable under the Equality Act 2010.

Security

New routes of attack

AI systems connected to email, documents or the web can be manipulated by instructions hidden in the material they read. They need the same security discipline as any other system.

Dependence

Cost, lock-in and de-skilling

Usage-based pricing can grow quickly, suppliers change their models without notice, and teams that stop checking the work lose the skill to spot when it is wrong.

“Artificial intelligence is a tool that carries with it risks as well as opportunities.” Dame Victoria Sharp P, R (Ayinde) v London Borough of Haringey [2025] EWHC 1383 (Admin), on lawyers who put AI-generated false citations before the court

How we implement

From Idea to Everyday Use

1

Opportunity map

We review how work flows through your business and identify where AI will genuinely save time or improve quality, and where it will not.

2

Risk and data review

What data would be used, where it would go, which rules apply, and what could go wrong, including a data protection impact assessment where needed.

3

Controlled pilot

A small, measured trial against real examples, with accuracy judged against a standard set before the pilot begins.

4

Safeguards

Human review at the right points, checks against source material, clear limits on what the system may decide, and a record of what it did.

5

Roll-out and training

Staff guidance, an AI use policy, and training so people know what the tools are for and when not to trust them.

6

Ongoing review

Periodic checks of accuracy, cost and risk, because models, suppliers and regulation all keep changing.
